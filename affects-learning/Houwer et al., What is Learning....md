**main idea**: explicitly define learning to assist cognitive learning research.

learning is changes in the behaviour of the organism resulting from regularities in the environment (ontogenetic - the development process -  adaptation)

basic definition of learning is: f(experience) = change in behaviour

counter-points:
1. learning may not result in change in behaviour (latent learning)
2. change of behaviour does not imply learning. hearing a loud bang and changing behaviour does not imply learning has occured.

author provided definition consists of 3 components:
- changes in behaviour
- regularities in environment
- causal relation between the two
so, f(regularities in environemnt) = changes in behaviour

author critiques mechanistic approaches to learning as difficult to work with, thereby emphasizing the functionality of their presented definition.
- Mechanistic discuss contiguous causes of learning, where learning immediately follows a change of behaviour
- mechanistic also discuss a change in the organism, which for experimental processes is referred to or defined.

author's new definition avoids counter-point #2 by specifying regularities in behaviour. so, a loud bang is not regular, and it would be excluded from definition.

author kinda avoids counter-point #1 by saying that the definition is kinda open-ended when it comes to latent learning, and that a change in behaviour is necessary to infer that learning has occured (for experiments).

for experimental purposes, the author discuss why their definition is easy to use, b/c you can introduce a regularity while keeping all other env elements constant to measure a change in behaviour
