#interdisciplinary #theoritical 

Learning is defined differently across different disciplines like psychology, sociology, computer science and biology. What if we can get one interdisciplinary definition for learning, while leaving fields to provide a more specific definition of learning for their own use-cases?

**main definition:** Learning is:
1. a system's processing of information from environment, that
2. results in a change in the system's properties, which
3. alters its behaviour potential and reactions to this information

authors compare and contrast functional and structural approaches to learning:
- functional view learning as having an effect on behaviour
- structural view learning as having an effect on internal state
	- reminds me of Houwer's comparison of mechanistic vs functional. The authors even cite his article in that section

functional definition has advantage of not having to know about a system's internal processes. buuuut its emphasis on behavioural change means it doesn't account for latent learning ("behavioural silence" problem). the authors classify it as more of a definition of learned behaviour. 

the authors thus present their definition as a way of combining structural and functional approaches.
- they use the word system to include organisim and machines
- they include the consensus that learning is a process, not an ability and not a result
- change in system's properties is a reference to structural lens
- alters its behaviour potential solves the behavioural silence problem
- reactions to this information is from a functional lens

--- 
i like the idea of an interdisciplinary definition but i think its mostly going to be a thing for academics to reason with. i feel like the point of a definition is to operationalize it, and this doesn't really help with that. it provides a base for other academics to specify the definition and operationalize it for their own use-cases

the discussion between structural vs functional is a reference to psychology's older schools of thought, and it helped ground the different lenses to learning
