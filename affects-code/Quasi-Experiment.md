#definition 
"quasi" meaning resembling. Not a true experiment, short of it. somewhere between correlation studies and true experiments

it has different variations and it's often used when you can't establish a control group or randomize study trials. so, you can't eliminate confounding variables

example: stress levels before and after a hurricane. You can't really select a control group and another to receive hurricane treatment. so you might get folks from different geographic areas.

some variations include:
- post-test only with control group: there's a group that  gets intervention, and a control group that doesnt. they are both measured after intervention
- pretest, postest with control group: 2 groups (not randomly assigned) are selected, and they are measured before intervention. then intervention is given to the experimental group. they are then measured after intervention
- one-group pretest, posttest: one group is measured before intervention, then given intervention then measured after intervention. doesnt account for historical events (an events that take place and influence study) and maturation (normal events that happen in the time between pretest and postest)
